building DAIŌDO

/// about ///

Science Weapon DAIŌDO (科学兵器ダイオード) is a television format and production pipeline for TV-style episodic filmmaking, currently under development.

/// logline ///

For fifteen years, humanity has lived under attack from hellish beings launching from a dark region on the surface of the Sun. With conventional defences powerless against the attackers, humanity’s last hope is DAIŌDO: an experimental weapon capable of transforming three women into a single 120-metre superhuman. - They are DAIŌDO Hīrō.

/// repo under construction ///

I’ve been working on Science Weapon DAIŌDO for about six weeks now. The project was born partly from the ashes of a few earlier experiments.

DAIŌDO developed directly out of what I learned during those projects: what can be generated reliably, what becomes prohibitively expensive or time-consuming, where continuity starts falling apart, where the tools are genuinely useful, and where one has to build tools specifically for the task at hand.

I’ve increasingly found myself borrowing the logic of limited animation: reusing locations, poses and assets; deciding where movement actually matters; letting sound and editing carry things that don’t need to move; and spending the difficult work on the shots where it counts. Early television anime and Hanna-Barbera are obvious influences on that thinking. Of the legacy forms, animation has much more in common with this workflow than CGI filmmaking does. 3D has nevertheless become essential, particularly for sets and spatial reference.

DAIŌDO, then, is born equally out of production necessity and a large dose of affection and nostalgia for the super robot and tokusatsu formats of bygone eras, particularly the foundational productions.

This database will serve as a public production record of the whole journey, from idea and script through development to final master. I’ve decided to develop the production publicly, with material appearing here as aspects of the project become locked and enter canon, for two reasons.

Where I find something useful, build something useful, hit a wall or discover that an approach is a complete waste of time, I intend to document it.

This record is intended to establish a dated public trail of the development and provenance of the project: its ideas, designs, aesthetics and original tools where applicable.

IP considerations will consequently appear in the production notes as I encounter them. That is new ground too, and seems worth documenting as part of the same process rather than pretending it exists separately, somewhere downstream.

There is no way to put a reliable timeframe on a finished pilot episode for DAIŌDO, but let’s put a flag in the ground: first deadline, 15 September 2027 for episode 00, with the aim of putting the pipeline in place and eventually being able to knock out a new episode each month, perhaps?

Whatever the current zeitgeist might suggest, making a film this way is not a matter of telling AI what you want and receiving a movie a few moments later. Once the aim is to make something that stands on its own rather than simply showcase the render model, you very quickly run into all the old problems of filmmaking and a collection of entirely new ones.

The renders are photoreal now. Fine. We can move on.

The production notes assume a reasonably solid foundation in filmmaking, animation.
